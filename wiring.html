Raspberry Pi 3B v1.2 · 40-pin header · BCM numbering in code

Dndboard Wiring

Every connection for the 6×14 key matrix, the LCD 1602A on I2C and the EC11 encoder. 25 of 26 GPIO pins are used; GPIO 8 is the only spare. Physical pin numbers are what you count on the header; GPIO numbers are what soundboard.py uses.

Connections by part

One key, with its diode

Rows are pulled up to 3.3 V inside the Pi. The code pulls one column low at a time and reads which rows drop. Current flows row → switch → diode → column, so the anode faces the row and the cathode stripe faces the column. Every one of the 84 keys is wired the same way.

Before the first power-on

The LCD backpack can put 5 V on the Pi's I2C pins.

The PCF8574 board runs from 5 V (pin 2), and most of them have 4.7 kΩ pull-up resistors from SDA and SCL to that 5 V. Pi GPIO pins are 3.3 V only. Many people run it like this without trouble, but it is out of spec. The safe options: remove the two pull-up resistors on the backpack (the Pi has its own 1.8 kΩ pull-ups to 3.3 V), or put a small I2C level shifter between them.

  1. Every diode faces the same way: stripe toward the column wire.
  2. With a multimeter, no row is shorted to another row, no column to another column, and nothing to 5 V or GND.
  3. Nothing is connected to pins 27 and 28 (ID EEPROM) or to the spare pin 24 unless you add something there.
  4. In sudo raspi-config → Interface Options: I2C on, SPI off, Serial Port with login shell off and serial hardware off.
  5. After boot, i2cdetect -y 1 shows the LCD at 27 (or 3f).
  6. Run uv run soundboard.py. If the volume goes the wrong way when you turn the knob, swap the A and B wires or swap 20 and 21 in the code.
